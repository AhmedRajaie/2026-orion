WEEK 2 · CONCEPT

Risk & Volatility

Two strategies can earn the same return and be nothing alike. The difference is risk.

01

Return is only half the story

Imagine two strategies that both turned 100 into 150. One climbed there smoothly. The other doubled, crashed to 60, and clawed back. Same final number — but you could never actually hold the second one; you'd have sold in a panic at 60. How you get there matters as much as where you end up.

02

Measuring the bumpiness

Volatility
The standard deviation of daily returns. High volatility means big swings up and down — more stress, more chance of being forced out at the worst time.
Max drawdown
The worst peak-to-trough fall along the way. A 50% drawdown means at some point you'd lost half. Few people stomach that.
Sharpe ratio
Return per unit of risk: how much reward you earned for the bumpiness you endured. Higher is better. The single number desks live by.
03

Why it changes how you build

Once you care about risk, 'maximize profit' stops being the whole goal. You start asking: can I get almost the same return with far fewer white-knuckle moments? That question is the seed of portfolio theory and of a good reward function for an RL agent.

Rule of thumb

A strategy with a great return and terrible drawdown is usually worse than a modest one that's steady. Survivability beats bravado.

Take this with you

Judge a strategy by return AND risk. A number you could never actually hold through isn't a real return.
