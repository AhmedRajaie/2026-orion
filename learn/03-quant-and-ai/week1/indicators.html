Indicators

A raw price is just a number. An indicator turns a stream of prices into a signal you can act on.

01

Why indicators exist

A price on its own tells you almost nothing. Is 18.50 high or low? Rising or falling? Calm or wild? An indicator is a simple calculation over recent prices that answers one of those questions with a number. You already know the idea from everyday life: a moving average is just a running mean, the way you might average your last few electricity bills to see the trend through the noise.

02

The four you'll build

SMA — Simple Moving Average
The average of the last N prices, recalculated each day. Smooths out daily noise so a trend becomes visible. A short SMA rising above a long SMA is a classic 'uptrend' signal.
EMA — Exponential Moving Average
Like the SMA, but recent days count more than old ones. It reacts faster to a change in direction.
RSI — Relative Strength Index
A 0–100 gauge of how hard price has been pushed up vs down lately. Above ~70 is often called 'overbought', below ~30 'oversold'.
Volatility
The standard deviation of returns — how much price bounces around. Your first real measure of risk.
03

From indicator to decision

Indicators are the raw material of a strategy. A rule as simple as 'hold the stock when its short average is above its long average' is a complete trading strategy — and it's the one you build in week 1.
