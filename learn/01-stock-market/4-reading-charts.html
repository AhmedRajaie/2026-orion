01 — CANDLESTICKS

How to Read a Single Candle

A candlestick is the densest piece of information in finance. In one shape, you can see a day's worth of price action — where it opened, where it closed, the highest and lowest points it touched, and whether buyers or sellers won. Once you can read one candle, you can read a chart.

↳ Anatomy of a candle
BULLISH (GREEN)
Close higher than open. Buyers won.
BEARISH (RED)
Close lower than open. Sellers won.

Candles tell stories

The shape of a candle reflects what happened during the period. A long body means a strong, decisive move. Long wicks mean the price went somewhere — but came back. The combination tells you who was in control and who got rejected. Here are four candles every trader recognises:

DOJI
Open ≈ Close. The market couldn't decide. Often a turning point — indecision before a move.
HAMMER
Sellers pushed price way down, then buyers pushed it all the way back up. Bullish reversal signal at bottoms.
SHOOTING STAR
Buyers pushed price way up, then sellers pushed it all the way back. Bearish reversal signal at tops.
BULLISH ENGULFING
A green candle that completely "engulfs" the previous red one. Buyers took over decisively. Strong reversal signal.
↳ Honest caveat
A single candle is one data point — context matters more than the shape. A hammer at the top of an uptrend means almost nothing. A hammer after a long downtrend, near a support level, on high volume — that's worth paying attention to. Candles by themselves are noise. Candles in context are signal.
02 — TRENDS, SUPPORT & RESISTANCE

The Two Most Useful Concepts in TA

If you only ever learn two things from technical analysis, learn these. Trends tell you which direction the market is moving. Support and resistance tell you where it's likely to pause, bounce, or break. Together, they're the entire foundation of price-based trading.

Trends — three states only

A market is always in one of three states. Sounds obvious — but most retail mistakes come from trading against the trend, or pretending one exists when the market is actually flat.

UPTREND
Higher highs and higher lows. Each pullback bottoms higher than the last. Buyers are in control.
DOWNTREND
Lower highs and lower lows. Each rally peaks lower than the last. Sellers are in control.
SIDEWAYS / RANGE
Price oscillates between a ceiling and a floor. Neither side is winning. Most markets are sideways most of the time.

Support & Resistance

Price has memory. When a stock has bounced off the same level three times, traders watching the chart remember — and they place orders there next time. That collective memory creates the level. Self-fulfilling, but real.

PRICE BOUNCES OFF SUPPORT (FLOOR) AND RESISTANCE (CEILING) MULTIPLE TIMES
Support
A floor under price
A price level where buyers historically step in. The more times price bounces off it, the stronger it gets. When support breaks, it often becomes the new resistance — what was a floor becomes a ceiling.
Resistance
A ceiling above price
A price level where sellers historically step in. Often round numbers (50 EGP, 100 EGP) or recent highs. When resistance breaks, it often becomes the new support — old ceiling becomes the new floor.
03 — INDICATORS

The Three Indicators Every Trader Knows

Indicators are mathematical formulas applied to price to highlight things the eye might miss. There are hundreds. Most are variations on each other. These three — Moving Averages, RSI, and MACD — cover 80% of what retail traders actually use. Important caveat upfront: indicators lag the market. They tell you what already happened, not what will happen.

INDICATOR 01

Moving Averages (MA)

The average closing price over the last N days. Smooths out daily noise to reveal the underlying trend. The two most-watched: 50-day MA (medium-term trend) and 200-day MA (long-term trend).

Above the 200-MAGenerally considered a bull market for that stock.
Golden Cross50-MA crosses above 200-MA. Classic bullish signal.
Death Cross50-MA crosses below 200-MA. Classic bearish signal.
INDICATOR 02

RSI — Relative Strength Index

A 0–100 score that asks: has this stock been gaining or losing recently, and how aggressively? Above 70 means "overbought" — the rally may be exhausted. Below 30 means "oversold" — the selling may have gone too far.

RSI is most useful at extremes. A stock at RSI 75 in a strong uptrend can stay overbought for weeks — selling on RSI alone is a common rookie mistake.
INDICATOR 03

MACD — Moving Average Convergence Divergence

A momentum indicator. It compares two moving averages (typically 12-day and 26-day) and shows whether they're converging or diverging. When the lines cross, it's a signal that momentum is shifting.

Bullish crossoverThe faster MA crosses above the slower MA. Often used as a buy signal.
Bearish crossoverThe faster MA crosses below the slower MA. Often used as a sell signal.
↳ Don't get indicator-pilled
New traders often add 8 indicators to a single chart, hoping more is better. It's not — they're mostly measuring the same thing. Pick two or three, learn what they actually mean, and trust the chart's price action above any indicator. Indicators don't predict — they describe.
04 — CHART PATTERNS

Shapes That Traders Watch For

Certain chart shapes appear so often that they have names and reputations. Some traders swear by them. Others think they're glorified pareidolia — finding faces in clouds. The truth is probably in between: patterns work partly because they capture real market psychology, and partly because enough people watch them that they become self-fulfilling.

HEAD & SHOULDERS
Three peaks, the middle one highest. Considered one of the most reliable bearish reversal patterns. When price breaks below the "neckline," it often signals the end of the uptrend.
DOUBLE TOP (M)
Price hits the same high twice and fails to break through. Buyers gave up. Bearish reversal — often signals a meaningful top.
DOUBLE BOTTOM (W)
Price hits the same low twice and bounces. Sellers gave up. Bullish reversal — often signals a meaningful bottom.
ASCENDING TRIANGLE
Higher lows pressing up against a flat resistance. Buyers getting more aggressive. Often resolves with an upward breakout.
DESCENDING TRIANGLE
Lower highs pressing down against a flat support. Sellers getting more aggressive. Often resolves with a downward breakdown.
BULL FLAG
A sharp rally followed by a small downward channel — like a flag on a flagpole. Usually a continuation pattern: the trend resumes after a brief pause.
↳ Pattern recognition trap
The human eye is built to find patterns — even where none exist. Once you learn what a head-and-shoulders looks like, you'll see them everywhere. The real test is statistical: do these patterns produce reliable returns out-of-sample, after costs? Academic studies are mixed at best. Treat patterns as one piece of evidence, not as predictions.
05 — THE LIMITS OF TA

What Charts Can and Can't Tell You

Technical analysis has fierce believers and fierce skeptics — both with valid points. The honest position is that TA is a useful lens for some things and useless for others. Understanding the line between them is what separates a thoughtful trader from a chart-worshipper.

What charts are good for
Identifying the trendThe simplest, highest-value use. Are buyers or sellers in control right now?
Defining riskCharts give you objective levels (recent lows, support zones) where you know your idea is wrong. That's how you set stops.
Timing entriesEven if you decided to buy a stock based on fundamentals, the chart can tell you whether to buy now or wait for a pullback.
Reading sentimentVolume spikes, gaps, climactic candles — these tell you when fear or euphoria is peaking.
What charts can't do
Predict newsAn earnings miss, a regulatory change, a CEO scandal — these aren't on the chart until after they hit.
Tell you what's expensiveA chart can't tell you a company's earnings or assets. A stock at all-time highs might still be undervalued; one at all-time lows might be a value trap.
Work in choppy marketsMost TA assumes there's a trend to ride. In sideways or noisy markets, indicators flip-flop and patterns fail constantly.
Eliminate emotionThe chart looks objective, but you're still making subjective calls. Confirmation bias survives any indicator.
↳ Why TA works (when it works)
TA isn't magic. When it works, it works for two reasons. First — self-fulfilling prophecy. If enough traders watch the 200-day moving average, they'll buy when price approaches it from above. That collective behaviour is what makes the level "support." Second — behavioural patterns. Markets are made of humans, and humans react predictably to the same emotional triggers. Fear at the bottom, greed at the top. Charts capture the footprint of those reactions. When TA fails, it's usually because something exogenous (news, regulation, macro shock) overwhelms the patterns. The patterns don't disappear — they just stop dominating.
↳ The honest takeaway
TA is a lens, not a crystal ball. The best traders use it alongside fundamentals (the lens we cover in Session 5), risk management, and humility about what they don't know. A chart pattern by itself is not a reason to trade. A chart pattern plus a thesis, a defined risk, and the willingness to be wrong — that's a setup.
