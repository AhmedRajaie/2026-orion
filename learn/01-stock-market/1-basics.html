Perspective 03 of 05

Or maybe it's just beautiful chaos — impossible to predict.

The Efficient Market Hypothesis argues that all known information is already priced in. No analysis can consistently beat the market, because prices move randomly in response to new, unpredictable information. If this is true, then the best strategy is simply: own everything, pay nothing in fees, and wait. Most active funds lose to the index over 10 years.

Perspective 04 of 05

It's an information game.
Who knows what, first.

Markets are a competition between participants with asymmetric information. Insiders know the earnings before you do. Big institutions pay for satellite imagery, credit card transaction flows, and sentiment data feeds. Fast traders see your order before it fully executes. The edge is always about information — its quality, speed, and interpretation.

01 — FOUNDATIONS

What Is the Stock Market?

At its core, a stock market is a marketplace where buyers and sellers trade ownership pieces of companies.

🏢
A Company Needs Capital
A company needs money to grow. Instead of borrowing from a bank, it can sell small pieces of itself — called shares — to the public.
🎟️
You Buy a Share
When you buy a share, you own a tiny fraction of that company. If the company does well, your share is worth more. If it does badly, it's worth less.

03 — ORDER TYPES

How Do You Buy or Sell?

When you trade, you place an "order" — an instruction to buy or sell. Different order types give you different control.

🎯
Market Order
Buy NOW at whatever the market offers. Guarantees execution, not price. Used when speed matters.
📍
Limit Order
Only buy at EGP 65 or lower. Guarantees price, not execution. The trade only happens if market reaches your target.
🛑
Stop-Loss Order
If price falls to EGP 55, automatically sell to cut losses. Critical for risk management — lets you sleep at night.
📈
Stop-Limit Order
Triggered at the stop price, but only executes within your limit range. More control, but can miss in fast markets.

04 — READING CHARTS

What Is a Price Chart?

A price chart is a visual record of a stock's price over time. It's the primary tool traders use to understand market behaviour. There are several types — each shows different levels of information.

The Line Chart

The simplest price chart. It connects only the closing prices of each period — one dot per day (or hour, or minute), joined by a line.

Good for seeing the big picture trend. But it throws away a lot of information — you don't see what happened during each period.

  • Clean and easy to read
  • Great for long-term trend analysis
  • Less noise, clearer direction
  • Shows only close — ignores open, high, low
  • Misses intraday volatility

NOTE: Volume is independent of chart type — it's always displayed as a separate bar subplot below any price chart.

The Japanese Candlestick

Invented in 18th-century Japan by rice trader Munehisa Homma. Each candle packs 4 data points into one visual: Open, High, Low, Close.

The body shows open-to-close. The wicks (thin lines above and below) show the highest and lowest price reached during that period. This is where the story is.

  • 4 values per candle: O, H, L, C
  • Wicks reveal rejection zones — key for TA
  • Candle shapes encode market psychology
  • More complex to read at first
  • Requires pattern knowledge to interpret

Price and volume are two separate dimensions of information. Neither tells the full story alone.
Volume measures how many shares changed hands in a given period. It answers: how much conviction was behind this price move?
● Green volume bar = price closed higher than previous day — buyers were in control.
● Red volume bar = price closed lower than previous day — sellers were in control.
A large price move on high volume is a strong signal. The same move on low volume is weak and likely to reverse.

06 — GLOSSARY

Key Terms

Essential vocabulary you'll encounter constantly. Know these before Session 2.

Bull Market
A market trending upward — prices rising 20%+ from recent lows. Optimism dominates.
Bear Market
A market trending downward — prices falling 20%+ from recent highs. Pessimism dominates.
Liquidity
How easily you can buy or sell without moving the price. High liquidity = easy to trade.
Volatility
How much price fluctuates. High volatility = bigger swings = more risk and opportunity.
Bid / Ask Spread
Bid = highest buyers offer. Ask = lowest sellers accept. The spread is the market maker's profit.
Market Cap
Total value of a company = share price × number of shares outstanding.
Dividend
A portion of company profits paid to shareholders. Not all companies pay dividends.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares to sell now, hoping to buy cheaper later. Betting on a price decline.
IPO
Initial Public Offering — when a private company sells shares to the public for the first time.
