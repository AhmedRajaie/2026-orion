01 — ASSET CLASSES

What Can You Actually Trade?

The market is not one thing. It's a collection of asset classes with different mechanics, participants, risk profiles, and time horizons.

📈
Equities
Stocks
Ownership stakes in companies. Buy a share, own a fraction of the business — its profits, assets, and future. The most familiar asset class and where most retail investing begins. Long-term stock investing is positive-sum because companies create real value.
EGX · NYSE · LSE
📄
Fixed Income
Bonds
You lend money — to a government or a company — for a fixed period, by buying a bond: a security, like a share, with a face value and a maturity date. In return, you get paid interest along the way, and your money back at the end. Most people access this through a bank savings certificate or a bond fund, rather than buying individual bonds directly.
OTC · Bond Markets
💱
Currency
Forex
Trading one currency against another — USD/EGP, EUR/USD. The largest market in the world at $7.5 trillion/day. Runs 24/5. Dominated by banks and central banks. Highly leveraged. Every dollar you gain is exactly a dollar someone else loses.
$7.5T/day · 24/5
📦
Derivatives
Futures
Contracts to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a future date. Both buyer and seller are obligated — no walking away. Originally for commodities (wheat, oil). Now covers indices, currencies, crypto. Leverage amplifies gains and losses equally.
Oil · Gold · Indices
⚙️
Derivatives
Options
The right — not the obligation — to buy (call) or sell (put) an asset at a set price before a set date. Unlike futures, the buyer's maximum loss is the premium paid. Asymmetric risk profile. More complex than futures. Deep session dedicated to options coming later.
Calls · Puts · Greeks
₿
Digital Assets
Crypto
Decentralised digital assets on blockchain. Bitcoin, Ethereum, and thousands of others. 10%+ daily moves are normal. Runs 24/7/365 — no circuit breakers, no closing time. Whether crypto is positive or zero-sum is genuinely debated.
24/7/365 · No halts

What Is an ETF?

Exchange-Traded Fund

A basket of assets — stocks, bonds, commodities — packaged as a single security that trades on an exchange like a stock. It tracks an index automatically. Buy one share of an S&P500 ETF and you own a tiny fraction of all 500 companies at once.

The hard truth about beating the market: Morningstar's Active/Passive Barometer found only 38% of active fund managers beat their passive peers in 2025, and just 21% did over the past decade. Full-time professionals — with research teams, real-time data, and decades of experience — mostly can't beat a simple index fund that costs almost nothing. That's the bar.

WHY TIMING MATTERS ON EGX
Asian close → EGX openTokyo and other Asian markets close before EGX opens. Their direction signals global risk appetite for the day.

Opening auction (09:30–10:00)Pre-market orders accumulate before the bell. The first official price reflects all overnight news at once — often the most volatile, most informative moment of the day.
04 — MARKET STRUCTURE

How Companies Get Into the Market

Every stock trading on EGX got there through a specific process. Understanding that process — and the difference between primary and secondary markets — is essential for making sense of IPOs, capital raises, and why the same shares can have very different histories.

Primary Market
Company → Investor
The company itself sells new shares directly to investors. The cash goes to the company to fund growth. Happens once at IPO, then again at each follow-on offering or rights issue. This is when EGX-listed companies actually raise capital.
Secondary Market
Investor → Investor
Existing shares change hands between investors on the exchange. The company gets none of this money — it's just shares moving between holders. This is what 99% of EGX trading actually is.
↳ The Lifecycle of a Listed Company
STAGE 01
Private
Founders and early investors own the company. Shares aren't tradable publicly.
STAGE 02
IPO
Initial Public Offering. Company sells shares to public investors for the first time. Investment banks underwrite, regulators approve.
STAGE 03
Listed & Trading
Shares trade freely on EGX. Company files quarterly disclosures, holds annual meetings, complies with FRA rules.
STAGE 04
Capital Events
Rights issues, stock splits, dividends, buybacks, secondary offerings. Each one changes either share count or share value.
STAGE 05
Delisting
Acquisition, take-private, or regulatory removal. Company exits the public market — voluntarily or forced.
Main Market
EGX 30, EGX 70, EGX 100. Established companies meeting full listing requirements: minimum capital, free float thresholds, 3-year financial track record, audited disclosures. Most household names trade here.
Nilex (SME Market)
EGX's market for small and medium-sized enterprises. Lower listing thresholds, lighter disclosure requirements, designed to give growing companies access to public capital before they're ready for the main board.
↳ Why this matters for you as an investor
When you buy a share on EGX, you're almost always trading in the secondary market — buying it from another investor, not from the company. The company doesn't receive that money directly, and the price is set by what other investors are willing to pay right now, not by the company's last fundraise. But a rising price still matters to the company: it makes future fundraising cheaper, improves borrowing terms, and strengthens its position for acquisitions or employee compensation. The connection isn't direct cash today — it's a stronger hand tomorrow.
05 — INFORMATION & PRICE

How Information Moves Price

Price changes when new information arrives. But not all information moves price equally — the speed of repricing depends on how public, how clear, and how actionable that information is. Understanding this is the bridge between "markets exist" and "why do prices move."

⚡
Hard Public News — Earnings, Macro Data
PRICED IN: SECONDS TO MINUTES
Company earnings beats, CPI prints, Fed rate decisions, GDP numbers. The moment it's released, prices move within seconds. Professional traders and automated systems read these instantly — by the time you've finished reading the headline, the price has already adjusted. On EGX, this window is slightly longer due to lower automation, but it's still measured in seconds, not minutes.
Speed of market reaction ●●●●●
📰
Soft Information — Analyst Reports, Ratings, News Flow
PRICED IN: HOURS TO DAYS
Analyst upgrades/downgrades, sector news, management commentary, regulatory changes. These take time to be read, interpreted, and acted on. Slower to be priced in than hard news — especially on EGX where analyst coverage is thin and fewer professional eyes are watching the news flow at any given moment.
Speed of market reaction ●●●○○
💬
Sentiment & Social Signals — Market Mood, Retail Flow
PRICED IN: DAYS TO WEEKS
Social media sentiment, retail investor enthusiasm, Google Trends for stock names, put/call ratios. Diffuse, noisy, hard to act on directly. But over days and weeks, the collective mood pushes prices in one direction or another — and shifts in that mood often precede big moves.
Speed of market reaction ●●○○○
🔭
Alternative Data — Satellite, Credit Card, Foot Traffic
PRICED IN: DAYS TO MONTHS (IF EVER)
Hedge funds pay for satellite images of parking lots to estimate retail sales before earnings. Credit card transaction data to predict revenue. App download trends to predict growth. These signals aren't in the price yet — they're leading indicators. Largely inaccessible for small players, but the concept is important: any data correlated to future earnings that isn't yet priced is an alpha source.
