Neural Nets, Without the Math

A neural network is a flexible function that learns from examples. That's the whole idea. No calculus required.

01

What it really is

Forget the brain metaphors. A neural network is just a function with adjustable knobs. You show it examples — 'given these features, the answer was this' — and it turns the knobs until its guesses match the answers. That's training. Nothing more mystical than tuning a radio until the station comes in clear.

Input
features
→
Knobs

→
Output

Learning
adjust knobs to reduce error
02

Layers and why 'deep'

Stack a few of these knob-layers and the function can represent more complicated patterns. 'Deep learning' just means 'several layers'. More layers can capture more — but they can also memorize noise, which brings us to the one thing you must always watch.

03

The trap: overfitting

Underfitting
The model is too simple — it misses the real pattern. Bad on training data AND new data.
Good fit
It learned the pattern that generalizes. Good on both.
Overfitting
It memorized the training data, including the noise. Looks brilliant on the past, fails on the future.
The habit that saves you

Always split your data: train on the early part, test on the later part the model never saw. If it's great on train but poor on test, it overfit. You'll do this on every model, every day of week 2.
