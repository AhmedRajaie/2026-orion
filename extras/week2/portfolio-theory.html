Portfolio Theory

Stop trying to pick the one winner. Decide how to spread your money — that's the real game.

01

Don't put all your eggs in one basket

The oldest advice in finance, and the most important. If you bet everything on one stock, you live and die by it. Spread across several that don't move in lockstep, and the bad days of one are cushioned by the good days of another. This is diversification, and it reduces risk almost for free.

02

The return–risk trade-off

Modern Portfolio Theory reframes the whole problem. Instead of 'which stock will go up?', it asks: given each asset's risk and how they move together, what mix gives the best return for a level of risk I can live with? The answer is a set of weights — how much of your money goes where.

03

A simple version you can build

The full theory uses expected returns and a covariance matrix. But a clean, honest first version is inverse-volatility weighting: put more money in the calmer assets, less in the wild ones. Risk drives the weights directly. That's the strategy you build on day 5 — and the bridge to reinforcement learning, which learns the weights instead of solving for them.
