Act II · It is alive

At every single price, total disagreement.

For you to buy, someone equally convinced has to sell. Every trade is two people who each believe the other is making a mistake.

And they are not calm about it. The market does not respond to facts. It responds to how people feel about facts.

Those feelings arrive in waves. They are the most reliable pattern in the whole system.

The centre of everything

The market is alive.

Find an edge. Trade it. Others notice. It gets crowded. It stops working.

This is not a metaphor. Profitable patterns are consumed by the people who find them. It reorganises itself around whatever you discover.

Act III · Your problem now

An algorithm is just a rule you can write down.

That is it. That is algorithmic trading. Everything after this is refinement, testing, and risk.

# when the short average crosses above the long one
fast = average(price, 10)
slow = average(price, 30)

if fast > slow:
    buy()
else:
    sell()
The superpower

Test a decade in ten seconds.

You can be wrong a thousand times before risking a single pound. Almost no other field lets you rehearse against reality this cheaply.

The trap

Everything works on the past.

Anyone can find a rule that fit yesterday perfectly. The actual problem — the whole problem — is finding one that survives tomorrow.

— FITTED ON THIS— NEVER SEEN THIS
Kill the perfectionism

You will lose constantly. And win anyway.

A good strategy is not a crystal ball. It is a probability engine. You can be wrong far more often than you are right and still come out well ahead, as long as you are right bigger than you are wrong.

So you never trust one run

Run it hundreds of times instead.

One backtest tells you nothing. Run it across hundreds of simulated markets and the question changes from “did it work?” to “how often, and how badly can it fail?”
